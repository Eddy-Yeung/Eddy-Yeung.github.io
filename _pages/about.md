Welcome! I am an Assistant Professor of Political Science in the [Bush School of Government & Public Service](https://bush.tamu.edu) at [Texas A&M University](https://www.tamu.edu). I was previously a Postdoctoral Researcher in the [Department of Politics & International Relations](https://www.politics.ox.ac.uk) at the [University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk).

My [research](https://eddy-yeung.github.io/research) examines how political communication and elite strategy shape (1) **democratic legitimacy under autocracy and democratic backsliding** and (2) the **psychology of conflict escalation and de-escalation**, with a focus on China, the United States, and their interactions. Uniting my scholarship is a broader interest in how elites and mass publics influence one another in the political system, both domestic and international.

I completed my PhD in Political Science at Emory University. I also hold an MPhil in Economic Research from the University of Cambridge, where I was [Hughes Hall Scholar](https://www.hughes.cam.ac.uk) and [Hong Kong Scholar](https://hkses.edb.gov.hk/en/index.html), and a BEcon in Economics, Politics, and Public Administration from the University of Hong Kong, where I was [John Swire Scholar](https://www.swire.com/en/community/scholarships.php), [Undergraduate Research Fellow](https://tl.hku.hk/urfp), and a first-generation-to-college student.

Growing up in Hong Kong's public housing shaped how I think about social inequality, social justice, and social policy. It also drew me closer to the masses, whose formation of political attitudes under elite influence is the crux of my research agenda.

My research has appeared in a wide range of journals, including [_Science_](https://www.science.org/journal/science), [_British Journal of Political Science_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-political-science), [_Comparative Political Studies_](https://journals.sagepub.com/home/cps), [_International Organization_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization), and [_The Journal of Politics_](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/toc/jop/current). I am also coauthor of [<em>Deescalating a Trade War</em>](https://www.amazon.com/Deescalating-Trade-War-Reciprocation-International/dp/1009659219) (Cambridge University Press, 2026), which examines the strategies of reciprocation amid US-China great power competition.

My theoretical innovations on mass–elite interactions on democracy and conflict have received **best paper and dissertation awards** from APSA's [Democracy and Autocracy](https://apsanet.org/membership/organized-sections/section35), [Political Communication](https://politicalcommunication.org/apsa-section), and [Political Psychology](https://connect.apsanet.org/s28) sections. My experimental research has been funded by the [Harry Frank Guggenheim Foundation](https://www.hfg.org), the [Mamdouha S. Bobst Center for Peace and Justice](https://bobst.princeton.edu), and the [U.S. National Science Foundation](https://www.nsf.gov), among others. I am the recipient of the [Distinguished Scholar Award](https://connect.apsanet.org/s28/awards) from APSA's Political Psychology section and the [Early Career Fellowship](https://connect.apsanet.org/s32) from APSA's Elections, Public Opinion, and Voting Behavior section.
